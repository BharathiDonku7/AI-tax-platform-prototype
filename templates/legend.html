Rules the system follows

  • 01 Traceability is row-level, not coordinate-level. A field points at a document, a page, and a specific row ID. The viewer renders that page's real content and highlights the row. A bounding box drawn over a scanned image breaks the moment the document is re-rendered; a row reference does not.
  • 02 State is never encoded by color alone. Every state carries a border, a chip, and a text label, so the system survives color blindness and grayscale printing — which matters when a return package gets printed for a client meeting.
  • 03 Locked always explains itself. A disabled field with no reason is the most common source of support tickets. Every locked field carries a sentence telling the user what to change instead.
  • 04 Confidence is banded, not decimal. The panel leads with "Low confidence," not "0.61." A raw score implies a precision the model doesn't have, and CPAs will anchor on it.
  • 05 Correcting the AI never erases it. The original suggestion, the correction, the reason, and the reviewer stay in the record. Under Sec. 280E audit scrutiny, that history is the substantiation.
  • 06 Derived values show their chain, not a number. Clicking a calculated field replaces the document viewer with its full derivation, and each step is itself clickable back to its own source page. Pointing a derived value at one document would be a lie about where it came from.
  • 07 Unindexed pages say so. The lease has 12 pages; two were authored. Navigating to page 9 says "not indexed in this prototype" rather than rendering invented content. Showing the seam honestly is better than hiding it.
  • 08 Accepting is one click. A preparer confirming a high-confidence extraction two hundred times a day should not have to open a dialog. Correcting — the rarer, higher-stakes action — is the one that asks for a reason.
